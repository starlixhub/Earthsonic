About EarthSonic

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Sonifying 47 Years of Earth Observation

EarthSonic bridges planetary science and acoustic perception by transforming 47 years of continuous observations from NASA and NOAA satellite instruments into multi-layered musical arrangements. By translating continuous empirical records into calibrated audio frequencies, listeners intuitively perceive planetary shifts across half a century of Earth system telemetry.

The Four Primary Data Channels

  • NASA GISTEMP v4: Global surface temperature anomaly records (1880–2026, calibrated against the 1951–1980 climatological baseline) drive the primary melodic synthesizer voice. Positive thermal excursions modulate upward pitch inflections and drive dynamic atmospheric day/night terminator displacement across the 3D globe.
  • Mauna Loa Observatory: Continuous atmospheric carbon dioxide mole-fraction measurements from the Keeling Record (336 to 426+ ppm) govern harmonic overtone saturation, atmospheric scattering haze, and resonant chorus depth, reflecting the relentless accumulation of greenhouse forcing.
  • NOAA NCEI Ocean Heat Content: Global ocean heat content in the 0–2000m layer represents over 90% of excess Earth system energy. This volumetric thermal surge drives a deep resonant sub-bass drone and controls a dynamic low-pass acoustic filter coupled with ocean specular glint shaders.
  • NSIDC Sea Ice Extent: Continuous satellite passive microwave observations of polar cryosphere extent from 1979 through 2026 are translated into high-frequency crystalline percussive textures and modulate polar ice cap surface albedo and surface bump shaders.

Algorithmic audio synthesis is powered by the STRAUSS (Sonification Tools and Resources for Astronomers Using Sound Synthesis) framework. Multi-decadal climate time series are synthesized into synchronized audio stems with strictly deterministic mathematical mappings, ensuring every sonic event reflects verified scientific observations without synthetic embellishment.
